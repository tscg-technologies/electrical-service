HAZARD RECOGNITION

Critical Situations Requiring Immediate Isolation

If you encounter any of the following 8 hazardous symptoms, immediately cease using connected equipment and follow safe precautions.

Burning Smell

Pungent ozone or burning plastic odor near wall switches, ceiling fixtures, or distribution panel boards indicating severe overheating.

Sparks From Outlets

Visible arcing, popping sounds, or persistent flashes when plugging or unplugging cords, signaling loose terminals or degraded contacts.

Repeated Breaker Trips

A circuit breaker or RCD safety switch that immediately trips upon resetting indicates a persistent short circuit or critical insulation breakdown.

Exposed Damaged Wires

Chewed, crushed, severed, or aged cabling showing bare copper conductors represents an extreme electrocution and fire hazard.

Panel Overheating

Main switchboard enclosure that is noticeably hot to touch, humming loudly, vibrating, or emitting discoloration marks on breaker bodies.

Sudden Electrical Failures

Total partial-phase blackout within a building where neighbor lines remain lit, indicating an intake phase drop or main incoming terminal fault.

Water Exposure Near Gear

Flooding, ceiling plumbing leaks, or rainwater infiltration reaching switchboards, recessed downlights, or sub-floor junction boxes.

Damaged Installations

Accidental impact damage from drilling into hidden wall cables, vehicle collisions with outdoor bollards, or crushed meter enclosures.

Essential Safety Guidance & Precautions

PUBLIC SAFETY EDUCATION

When electrical hazards arise, life safety always supersedes convenience. Follow these mandatory safety practices:

Never touch exposed or damaged electrical wires. Even neutral conductors or disconnected lines can carry lethal back-feed voltage.

Do not attempt to repair electrical panels yourself. Distribution boards contain uninsulated live busbars that bypass standard household breakers.

Keep people and pets away from sparking or damaged equipment. Secure the room and isolate access until verified safe.

If there is smoke or fire, evacuate immediately and contact your national emergency fire services from a safe outdoor distance.

Do not touch electrical equipment that may be wet or energized. Water greatly accelerates conductivity and electrocution risk.

Where safe to do so, keep away from the affected area and seek qualified, licensed professional assistance.
